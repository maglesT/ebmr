   The plan already expects a human-readable rendering: the outline's archival section names a "canonical PDF/A-3
   rendering", and ADR 0010 rejected frozen JSON alone because it is "not a human-readable true copy". There is no
   page today that reads like a batch manufacturing record.

   2. What to build

   One Batch record page per batch that tells the whole story, from the ERP order to the release (owner, 28 Oct:
   "from ERP order to release, all details should be there"). It covers the processing record and its packaging order(s)
   together, on one scrolling page, in the order things happened. Every item shows who did it, when, and the signature
   with its meaning.

          1. Header:
                  product, strength, batch number, batch size;
                     MBR family, version and revision;
                     markets, made and expiry dates;
                     status, review state, release state;
                     the record's content hash.
          2. ERP order: order number and ERP reference, product, quantity, the order's BOM, when it was received, and
             the BOM-versus-MBR check.
          3. Planning and batch issue: the batch number allocated (rule and serial), who planned and issued it, the MBR
             pinned, and when.
          4. Materials:
                   every lot reserved: lot, material, quantity, its status and expiry, who reserved it;
                     for an API, its assay, water and as-is potency at the time;
                     later, what was actually consumed from each lot.

   3. View only: no download

            It is a screen view: no download, export or "save as PDF" button on it.
            Printing is turned off for this page: a print stylesheet that shows only "Printing is not available; use the
            Archive for a controlled true copy". Screenshots cannot be prevented, so every screen carries a watermark
            with the viewer's name and the time, plus "Controlled view, not a copy".
            Reading it needs a record-view permission held by the review, QA, production-head and QC roles. Floor roles
            see only their own batch, and a contract giver only its loan-licence batches.
            Opening the record is written to the access log (who viewed which record and when).
            A batch that is still running shows the record so far, with a red "IN PROGRESS: not final" band. A locked
            record shows "Final, locked on …".


      Keep the controlled copy for inspectors. The regulations require a site to provide accurate and complete
      copies of records, both human-readable and electronic, for inspection. The Archive true-copy export therefore
      stays, but only for the archive and QA roles, with a reason and an audit entry. It should use the same readable
      layout as this view, instead of the raw rows it has today (the record's data stays embedded, as now). Only the
      everyday view has no download.

   R12 · Dissolution as an in-process test

   Today: dissolution exists only as a lab release test on the finished lot (specification test, two-stage profile, slice 7p). No
   MBR block records dissolution during manufacture. Disintegration is an in-process block (rabeprazole step 5.2.11).
   Owner: dissolution should also be possible as an in-process test.

   Why plants need it:

            the acid-resistance check of enteric-coated cores before they are released to packing;
            a dissolution check of sustained-release cores before coating;
            a check after a coating-level change or a reprocessing step.

   The result decides whether the batch goes on, so it must be in the batch record.

   What to build: a new tablet block, "In-process dissolution", that can be placed in any stage of an MBR.

         1. In the MBR:
                   apparatus (basket or paddle), medium, volume, temperature, speed;
                     the time points (one or several; two-stage acid → buffer for delayed release);
                     units per stage (6);
                     limits per point;
                     the staging rule of the pharmacopoeia cited (for example S1/S2/S3, or A1–A3 and B1–B3 for delayed
                     release, with the edition: IN-33);
                     sample size;
                     whether the batch waits for the result (hold point) or goes on at risk.
         2. Two ways to record, chosen in the MBR:
                 (a) on the floor or in the IPC lab: the analyst records the start (instrument ID, calibration status
                 checked at point of use), each unit's % released at each point, and the end. The block computes the
                 mean and the stage outcome. When a stage fails, it asks for the next stage's units.
                     (b) as a sample to QC: the step sends an in-process sample request (to LIMS-lite or the LIMS), and the
                     result comes back into the step. A hold point keeps the next stage closed until the approved result is in.
         3. Instrument data: the values can come from a dissolution tester or its software (adapter), or be typed with the
            manual-entry rules (second person, exception), the same as for balances.
         4. Results:
                  a failing result raises an exception and blocks the stage sign-off until it is dispositioned or a deviation is
                  linked;

                     the result shows in the step, the stage summary, the R11 record view and the review.

   R13 · See and change the potency before dispensing

   Owner's questions (25 Oct): "Can I see the potency calculation before dispensing?" and "I want to adjust the potency
   percentage." Today:

            the lot's potency (assay 99.40 %, water 0.60 %, as-is 98.80 %) and the calculated weights show nowhere
            before the weighing step;
            even there, the weights do not show (R10 #1);
            changing the potency % is possible: QC records a new ASSAY result, the head of QC approves it, QC records
            the lot assay with a reason, all before the operator presses Calculate. But the path is spread over several
            pages and no guide or screen explains it.

   What to build:

         1. Potency on the lot: Material lots → an API lot shows its current assay, water, basis, as-is potency, the results it
            came from, and its history of changes (who, when, why).
         2. Preview before dispensing: at lot reservation and on the batch's planning page, a "Potency preview" shows
            what TB-01 will calculate for the reserved lot(s): the active's target and the compensating excipient's target. It is
            marked "preview: the weighing step recalculates" and is never a recorded value.
         3. Change the potency %, one guided page for QC:
                 "Update the potency of lot X": choose the new approved ASSAY and WATER results, give a reason,
                 sign;
                     the preview updates;
                     if the batch has already calculated, the step shows "Potency changed since the calculation: recalculate",
                     and the change is an exception for QA;
                     typed values stay refused (results only), as now.
         4. Never let the floor type a potency % or a target weight. The controls stay as they are (PR-19, DI-33).
